Accounting for curtailment, self-consumption, and storage

Accounting for curtailment, self-consumption, and storage: a system of equations¶

Why this page exists¶

The wind-offshore workstream (see Wind offshore replication) found a persistent positive bias against SMARD’s actual generation that survives fixing capacity accounting (notebook 35) and correlates cleanly with system-wide renewable oversupply (notebook 36) — evidence for curtailment, but not proof, and not yet separated from the other candidate explanations (outages, model error) or connected to the solar workstream’s own open self-consumption question (26’s takeaways: bias and self-consumption share grow together over time, “can’t separate... from these two panels alone”). The wider project goal is a day-ahead residual-load forecast, which needs two things that are never directly observed: potential renewable generation net of curtailment, and gross (not just grid-metered) power demand.

This page fixes notation once, then writes down every equation relating potential generation, hidden losses, what’s actually observed, demand, and storage — as a single reference for what’s being estimated, from what, and why.

Notation¶

Indices¶

SymbolMeaning
tHour (the project’s native resolution throughout)
iTechnology index, i ∈ {wind_on, wind_off, pv}

Potential generation (hidden, modeled from weather + capacity)¶

SymbolMeaning
P_i(t)This project’s own modeled weather-driven technical potential for technology i, before any real-world loss
P_total(t)= P_wind_on(t) + P_wind_off(t) + P_pv(t), total renewable potential
P_pv,full_feed_in(t)PV potential attributable only to MaStR units registered under the full_feed_in category (zero self-consumption by regulatory construction)
P_pv,self_consumption(t)PV potential from the remaining (self-consumption-registered) units

Hidden losses (between potential and what’s actually produced)¶

SymbolMeaning
L_repair,i(t)Loss from technical unavailability: storm damage, cable faults, scheduled maintenance. Price- and grid-signal-independent
L_price,i(t)Voluntary curtailment during negative prices (not a grid-operator order, not in any published redispatch dataset)
L_congestion,i(t)Grid-operator-enforced curtailment (Redispatch 2.0 / Einspeisemanagement) — the one loss category with published official data, and only from October 2021
ε_i(t)This project’s own model error — P_i itself being wrong. Kept separate from the above on principle, even though empirically indistinguishable from real losses without independent validation
BTM_i(t)Behind-the-meter self-consumption of technology i’s own production. ≈0 for both wind technologies (Germany’s wind fleet is ~100% utility-scale, always grid-connected) — essentially a PV-only term

The produced-but-not-yet-gridded split (PV only)¶

SymbolMeaning
Produced_i(t)What’s physically generated after real-world losses, before the grid/behind-the-meter split
SelfUse_pv(t)PV output self-consumed in the same hour it’s generated
Charge_btm(t), Discharge_btm(t)Flows into/out of a behind-the-meter battery paired with rooftop PV
SOC_btm(t)Behind-the-meter battery state of charge — genuinely hidden; MaStR’s self_consumption_with_storage category flags which capacity has this arrangement, not its state at any hour

Observed (on the grid, from SMARD)¶

SymbolMeaning
G_i(t)Grid-injected generation for technology i
G_other(t)Grid-injected generation from everything dispatch-driven rather than weather-driven (fossil, biomass, hydro, nuclear until 2023, geothermal) — no “potential” modeled for this bucket
G_storage,charge(t), G_storage,discharge(t)Grid-scale storage (pumped hydro, increasingly grid batteries). Not hidden — SMARD already publishes this; not yet pulled into this project, but straightforward via pecdr/smard.py’s existing Variable enum
NetImport(t)Observed cross-border physical flow

Demand¶

SymbolMeaning
D_grid(t)SMARD’s published load: only what’s drawn from the public grid
D_btm(t)Behind-the-meter demand met by local self-consumption — invisible on both the generation and demand side simultaneously
D_true(t)Gross demand, = D_grid(t) + D_btm(t) — never directly observed

The demand model (estimated)¶

SymbolMeaning
D̂_true(t; θ)The fitted estimate of gross demand
dow(t)Day-of-week category
Holiday(t)Public-holiday indicator
T(t)Germany NUTS0 area-average 2m temperature (pecd_temperature_de.parquet) — PECD’s population-weighted product (TAW) exists but only at the pan-European “synchronous zone” level, unusable for isolating Germany, hence area-average here
trend(t)A slow-moving term for structural change over 2019-2025 (e.g. 2022’s demand destruction from the energy-price crisis)
kSelf-consumption coefficient: the fraction of P_pv(t) self-consumed rather than exported, as fit by the demand regression (or k_full_feed_in, k_self_consumption if fit separately per feed-in category — k_full_feed_in should come out ≈0 as a built-in falsification check)

The oversupply proxy and curtailment model¶

SymbolMeaning
Gap_i(t)= P_i(t) - G_i(t), the one directly computable quantity from this project’s existing outputs
OversupplyProxy_obs(t)= (G_wind_on+G_wind_off+G_pv)(t) / D_grid(t) — the proxy used in notebooks 22/36. Built from already-curtailed observed values, so it understates true oversupply exactly when curtailment is largest
OversupplyProxy_potential(t)= P_total(t) / D̂_true(t) — the corrected version, built from potential and gross demand instead, free of that attenuation bias
L̂(·)The fitted curtailment-vs-oversupply function, calibrated on wind’s own clean gap (no BTM term to contaminate it), then applied to PV

The forecasting target¶

SymbolMeaning
ResidualLoad(t)= D_grid(t) - Σ_i G_i(t), the realized/observed quantity
ResidualLoad_hat(t)= D̂_true(t) - (P_total(t) - L̂(t)), the forecastable estimate — everything on the right is knowable ahead of a day-ahead forecast from weather and calendar

The equations¶

1. Per-technology physical balance. What potential generation decomposes into: real-world losses plus whatever was actually produced, plus this project’s own model error.

Pi(t)=Lrepair,i(t)+Lprice,i(t)+Lcongestion,i(t)+Producedi(t)+εi(t)P_i(t) = L_{repair,i}(t) + L_{price,i}(t) + L_{congestion,i}(t) + Produced_i(t) + \varepsilon_i(t)Pi​(t)=Lrepair,i​(t)+Lprice,i​(t)+Lcongestion,i​(t)+Producedi​(t)+εi​(t)
(1)

2. The produced/behind-the-meter split. What’s physically produced either reaches the public grid or is used behind the meter.

Producedi(t)=Gi(t)+BTMi(t)Produced_i(t) = G_i(t) + BTM_i(t)Producedi​(t)=Gi​(t)+BTMi​(t)
(2)

For wind, BTM_i(t) ≈ 0 structurally — this is what made the offshore bias-driver analysis (notebook 36) a clean read on curtailment specifically, with no self-consumption confound to separate out.

3. Behind-the-meter self-consumption, with battery time-shifting (PV only). Same-hour self-use plus whatever a paired battery discharges, minus whatever it charges.

BTMpv(t)=SelfUsepv(t)+Dischargebtm(t)−Chargebtm(t)BTM_{pv}(t) = SelfUse_{pv}(t) + Discharge_{btm}(t) - Charge_{btm}(t)BTMpv​(t)=SelfUsepv​(t)+Dischargebtm​(t)−Chargebtm​(t)
(3)

4. Behind-the-meter battery state. The battery’s own stock-flow recursion — this is what turns self-consumption from a same-hour cancellation into a time-shifted one.

SOCbtm(t)=SOCbtm(t−1)+Chargebtm(t)−Dischargebtm(t)−lossbtm(t)SOC_{btm}(t) = SOC_{btm}(t-1) + Charge_{btm}(t) - Discharge_{btm}(t) - loss_{btm}(t)SOCbtm​(t)=SOCbtm​(t−1)+Chargebtm​(t)−Dischargebtm​(t)−lossbtm​(t)
(4)

5. System balance. An accounting identity, true by construction — not something to estimate. Whatever the grid draws must equal everything injected into it.

Dgrid(t)=∑iGi(t)+(Gstorage,discharge(t)−Gstorage,charge(t))+Gother(t)+NetImport(t)D_{grid}(t) = \sum_i G_i(t) + \big(G_{storage,discharge}(t) - G_{storage,charge}(t)\big) + G_{other}(t) + NetImport(t)Dgrid​(t)=i∑​Gi​(t)+(Gstorage,discharge​(t)−Gstorage,charge​(t))+Gother​(t)+NetImport(t)
(5)

6. Gross demand. Grid-metered load plus the invisible behind-the-meter piece.

Dtrue(t)=Dgrid(t)+Dbtm(t)D_{true}(t) = D_{grid}(t) + D_{btm}(t)Dtrue​(t)=Dgrid​(t)+Dbtm​(t)
(6)

D_btm(t) is met by BTM_i(t) first (mostly PV); any shortfall is drawn from the grid (already inside D_grid); any BTM surplus beyond local demand is either exported (counted in G_i) or wasted.

7. The gross-demand regression. This is the estimable link between the hidden demand side and what’s observed: grid-metered load equals a calendar/weather-driven demand model minus PV self-consumption, plus noise. Linear in every unknown, solvable with plain OLS — nighttime and heavily overcast hours (P_pv(t)≈0) pin down the demand-model coefficients with no self-consumption interference at all, since the -k·P_pv(t) term vanishes there; daytime hours (where P_pv(t) varies a lot at fixed calendar/weather conditions, due to cloud cover) then pin down k.

Dgrid(t)=β0+βdow(t)+βholiday ⁣⋅ ⁣Holiday(t)+ftemp(T(t))+trend(t)⏟D^true(t;θ)  −  k⋅Ppv(t)+ε(t)D_{grid}(t) = \underbrace{\beta_0 + \beta_{dow}(t) + \beta_{holiday}\!\cdot\!Holiday(t) + f_{temp}(T(t)) + trend(t)}_{\hat D_{true}(t;\theta)} \; - \; k \cdot P_{pv}(t) + \varepsilon(t)Dgrid​(t)=D^true​(t;θ)β0​+βdow​(t)+βholiday​⋅Holiday(t)+ftemp​(T(t))+trend(t)​​−k⋅Ppv​(t)+ε(t)
(7)

7a. Refinement: split by feed-in category. solar_capacity_by_pv_category_month already tracks which capacity is registered full_feed_in (required to export 100%, no self-consumption possible) versus self-consumption categories. Fitting separate coefficients gives a built-in falsification test.

Dgrid(t)=D^true(t;θ)−kfull_feed_in⋅Ppv,full_feed_in(t)−kself_consumption⋅Ppv,self_consumption(t)+ε(t)D_{grid}(t) = \hat D_{true}(t;\theta) - k_{full\_feed\_in} \cdot P_{pv,full\_feed\_in}(t) - k_{self\_consumption} \cdot P_{pv,self\_consumption}(t) + \varepsilon(t)Dgrid​(t)=D^true​(t;θ)−kfull_feed_in​⋅Ppv,full_feed_in​(t)−kself_consumption​⋅Ppv,self_consumption​(t)+ε(t)
(8)

k_full_feed_in should come out statistically indistinguishable from zero; if it doesn’t, that’s a sign of a modeling problem, not a real effect. (A further, not-yet-formalized refinement: self-consumption should saturate — a household can’t self-consume more than its own local demand — so a piecewise-linear or spline term in P_pv(t) is more physically honest than a single linear k, and still OLS-solvable.)

8. The naive oversupply proxy (what notebooks 22/36 actually used), and why it’s biased. Built entirely from observed values — which is exactly the problem, since at the hours with the most curtailment, the observed numerator is already suppressed by that same curtailment.

OversupplyProxyobs(t)=Gwind_on(t)+Gwind_off(t)+Gpv(t)Dgrid(t)OversupplyProxy_{obs}(t) = \frac{G_{wind\_on}(t) + G_{wind\_off}(t) + G_{pv}(t)}{D_{grid}(t)}OversupplyProxyobs​(t)=Dgrid​(t)Gwind_on​(t)+Gwind_off​(t)+Gpv​(t)​
(9)

9. The corrected, potential-based oversupply proxy. Curtailment decisions respond to the true oversupply situation, not the already-curtailed observed one — so the proxy driving L̂ should be built from potential and gross demand instead.

OversupplyProxypotential(t)=Ptotal(t)D^true(t)OversupplyProxy_{potential}(t) = \frac{P_{total}(t)}{\hat D_{true}(t)}OversupplyProxypotential​(t)=D^true​(t)Ptotal​(t)​
(10)

10. The curtailment model, calibrated on wind and extrapolated to PV. Wind’s own gap has no BTM term to separate out, so it’s a clean training signal for how curtailment responds to oversupply; applying that same fitted relationship to PV’s own oversupply hours predicts PV’s curtailment without needing to observe it directly.

Gapwind,i(t)≈Lrepair,i(t)+Lprice,i(t)+Lcongestion,i(t)+εi(t),L^(t)=L^(OversupplyProxypotential(t))Gap_{wind,i}(t) \approx L_{repair,i}(t) + L_{price,i}(t) + L_{congestion,i}(t) + \varepsilon_i(t), \qquad \hat L(t) = \hat L\big(OversupplyProxy_{potential}(t)\big)Gapwind,i​(t)≈Lrepair,i​(t)+Lprice,i​(t)+Lcongestion,i​(t)+εi​(t),L^(t)=L^(OversupplyProxypotential​(t))
(11)

10a. The residual left over for PV, once curtailment is subtracted out. Whatever remains of PV’s own gap after removing the wind-calibrated curtailment prediction is an estimate of PV’s self-consumption plus its own repair losses and model error.

Gappv(t)−L^pv(t)  ≈  BTMpv(t)+Lrepair,pv(t)+εpv(t)Gap_{pv}(t) - \hat L_{pv}(t) \;\approx\; BTM_{pv}(t) + L_{repair,pv}(t) + \varepsilon_{pv}(t)Gappv​(t)−L^pv​(t)≈BTMpv​(t)+Lrepair,pv​(t)+εpv​(t)
(12)

Cross-checkable against solar_capacity_by_pv_category_month: if this residual tracks the self-consumption-registered capacity share over time, that’s independent corroboration it’s really BTM, not ε or L_repair. L_repair and ε stay bundled throughout this whole chain — genuinely unidentifiable further without real outage data or an independently validated model, neither of which this project has.

11. The forecasting target. Realized residual load is a pure accounting identity from already-observed values — nothing to estimate. The forecastable version substitutes gross demand and potential-minus-estimated-curtailment for the observed quantities, so it can be computed ahead of time from weather and calendar alone.

ResidualLoad(t)=Dgrid(t)−∑iGi(t)ResidualLoad(t) = D_{grid}(t) - \sum_i G_i(t)ResidualLoad(t)=Dgrid​(t)−i∑​Gi​(t)
(13)
ResidualLoad^(t)=D^true(t)−(Ptotal(t)−L^(t))\widehat{ResidualLoad}(t) = \hat D_{true}(t) - \big(P_{total}(t) - \hat L(t)\big)ResidualLoad(t)=D^true​(t)−(Ptotal​(t)−L^(t))
(14)

Note that BTM doesn’t appear in the forecast formula at all — it cancels out of residual load exactly as it does in the realized version (the self-consumed MWh is absent from both published generation and published demand equally). It still earned its place in equation 7: without the -k·P_pv(t) term, D̂_true(t) itself would be biased by mistaking the solar self-consumption dip for a real demand pattern. BTM matters for getting the demand model right; it drops out of the final target.

A dead end worth flagging, not just a live lead¶

An earlier idea was to use realized fossil generation + net imports as a third, BTM-free channel to estimate L_price + L_congestion for the whole system independently of the per-technology gaps. Writing the system balance out at both potential and actual renewable output kills this:

Gaptotal(t)≡∑i(Pi(t)−Gi(t))=(Dgrid(t)−∑iPi(t))−(Dgrid(t)−∑iGi(t))Gap_{total}(t) \equiv \sum_i \big(P_i(t) - G_i(t)\big) = \big(D_{grid}(t) - \textstyle\sum_i P_i(t)\big) - \big(D_{grid}(t) - \sum_i G_i(t)\big)Gaptotal​(t)≡i∑​(Pi​(t)−Gi​(t))=(Dgrid​(t)−∑i​Pi​(t))−(Dgrid​(t)−∑i​Gi​(t))
(15)

which reduces to Gap_total(t) = Gap_total(t) — tautological. Without an actual dispatch/merit-order model, “counterfactual fossil+import” is defined as whatever the residual demands, so it adds no information beyond P and G directly. Fossil/import data isn’t worthless — it can show whether must-run constraints are binding (fossil pinned at a floor, forcing curtailment as the only release valve) — but that’s mechanism evidence, not a new decomposition axis, and shouldn’t be oversold as one.

What real congestion data can and can’t add¶

Checked directly by building a download pipeline against it, not just assumed: SMARD publishes monthly redispatch-adjusted feed-in volumes by energy carrier (wind onshore, wind offshore, solar, and the conventional sources), covering July 2022 onward — see notebook 44 for the full walkthrough and pecdr/smard_redispatch.py/pipeline/43_download_redispatch_by_source.py for how it’s fetched. That’s real ground truth for L_congestion specifically, per technology, but with real limits: (1) it starts July 2022, so it can’t cover this project’s full 2019-2025 backtest; (2) it’s monthly and national-only — no hour-of-day or grid-region detail; (3) checked directly (not assumed), it is not a clean redispatch-only figure — it’s TSO+DSO combined and folds in countertrading and grid-reserve volume alongside redispatch proper (see notebook 46); it never captures L_price (voluntary, market-based curtailment from negative prices) or L_repair (not a redispatch measure at all).

A second, per-measure source (netztransparenz.de’s live Redispatch export) does give real timestamps and per-plant names, but turned out to carry a clean technology label only for offshore wind — onshore wind and PV are almost entirely hidden behind anonymous substation/cluster codes, so it doesn’t supply the clean per-technology, per-hour recalibration input it initially looked like it would. See notebook 45 and notebook 46 for what it actually offers (mainly: attributable shape, not yet reliable absolute levels) and how the two sources’ totals were reconciled.

Because tomorrow’s curtailment is never observable at forecast time regardless, L̂(t) in equation 11 has to be an estimated function of forecastable inputs either way — real congestion data’s role is validating or recalibrating that estimated function against history, not supplying it a live input. The one thing this monthly data already makes checkable: it shows wind offshore curtailed roughly 8-9x more heavily per MW installed than onshore (see the congestion data chapter) — a real technology-specific asymmetry that the pooled onshore+offshore curtailment curve in notebooks 39/40/42 currently discards.
