What drives the gap against SMARD?

12_compare_all_approaches found that all three modeled series -- including PECD’s own official product (approach 1) -- systematically overshoot SMARD’s actual wind-onshore generation at high capacity factors. Since approach 1 isn’t derived from this project’s replication at all, that shared pattern is evidence the gap is a genuine physical-vs-grid effect (curtailment, high-wind shutdown, behind-the-meter self-consumption -- see ../markdown/index.md), not a defect specific to this project’s methodology.

This notebook digs into when the bias shows up, pooled across every year in pecdr.domain.FULL_YEAR_BACKTEST_YEARS, to see which candidate explanation it’s most consistent with:

  • Bias vs. CF level -- grows with output level, as expected if it’s driven by high-wind curtailment/shutdown rather than a flat offset.

  • Bias vs. month -- larger in winter (higher wind and more grid congestion) would point toward curtailment; a flat seasonal pattern would point toward something structural instead (e.g. a fleet-vintage or power-curve mismatch).

  • Bias vs. hour of day -- larger overnight, when demand is low and negative prices make curtailment more likely, would support the curtailment/oversupply story specifically (as opposed to shutdown, which has no time-of-day preference of its own).

  • Bias vs. renewable oversupply -- (wind + solar) / load as a rough proxy for how close the grid is to zero/negative residual load, the condition curtailment is most likely under.

  • Bias vs. year -- checks whether any of the above is stable across the backtest period, or whether one year is doing all the work.

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

from pecdr.capacity_weighting import capacity_weighted_cf, total_hourly_capacity
from pecdr.domain import FULL_YEAR_BACKTEST_YEARS
from pecdr.paths import ProjPaths

paths = ProjPaths()
paths.ensure_directories()

PERIOD_LABEL = f"{FULL_YEAR_BACKTEST_YEARS[0]}-{FULL_YEAR_BACKTEST_YEARS[-1]}"

Rebuild the same three series as 12_compare_all_approaches¶

mastr_peon_month = pd.read_parquet(paths.mastr_capacity_by_peon_month_file)

pecd_peon_cf = pd.read_parquet(paths.pecd_wind_onshore_capacity_factors_file)
approach1 = capacity_weighted_cf(pecd_peon_cf, mastr_peon_month)

approach3 = pd.read_parquet(paths.wind_onshore_full_year_cf_file)["national"]

target_panel = pd.read_parquet(paths.target_panel_file)
total_capacity_hourly = total_hourly_capacity(mastr_peon_month, target_panel.index)
smard_implied_cf = target_panel["wind_onshore"] / total_capacity_hourly

comparison = pd.DataFrame({
    "1_pecd_official_x_mastr": approach1,
    "3_pecd_replication": approach3,
    "4_smard_implied": smard_implied_cf,
}).loc[FULL_YEAR_BACKTEST_YEARS[0]:FULL_YEAR_BACKTEST_YEARS[-1]].dropna()

comparison["diff1"] = comparison["1_pecd_official_x_mastr"] - comparison["4_smard_implied"]
comparison["diff3"] = comparison["3_pecd_replication"] - comparison["4_smard_implied"]

# Renewable-oversupply proxy: (wind + solar generation) / load, from SMARD.
# High values approximate the low/negative-residual-load conditions under
# which curtailment is most likely to bind.
renewables = target_panel.loc[comparison.index, ["pv", "wind_onshore", "wind_offshore"]].sum(axis=1)
comparison["renewable_share_of_load"] = renewables / target_panel.loc[comparison.index, "load"]

print(f"Hours compared: {len(comparison):,}")
print(f"Approach 1 vs SMARD -- bias: {comparison['diff1'].mean():.4f}, MAE: {comparison['diff1'].abs().mean():.4f}")
print(f"Approach 3 vs SMARD -- bias: {comparison['diff3'].mean():.4f}, MAE: {comparison['diff3'].abs().mean():.4f}")

1. Does the bias grow with capacity-factor level?¶

Both approaches shown side by side against the same SMARD-implied CF bins -- if the shared shape survives even though approach 1 and approach 3 arrive at their numbers completely differently, that’s further evidence the effect lives in the SMARD side of the comparison (curtailment/ shutdown), not in either model.

bins = np.linspace(0, comparison["4_smard_implied"].max(), 21)
comparison["cf_bin"] = pd.cut(comparison["4_smard_implied"], bins)
bin_mid = [interval.mid for interval in comparison["cf_bin"].cat.categories]

bin_stats1 = comparison.groupby("cf_bin", observed=True)["diff1"].mean().reindex(comparison["cf_bin"].cat.categories)
bin_stats3 = comparison.groupby("cf_bin", observed=True)["diff3"].mean().reindex(comparison["cf_bin"].cat.categories)

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(9, 5))

Takeaways¶

  • CF level and month: both patterns from the single-year (2020) pass hold up over the full 2019-2025 backtest, and come out cleaner with 7x the sample -- bias grows roughly monotonically with SMARD CF, and is largest in the high-wind months (Jan/Feb, Oct-Dec), near zero in summer. Both approaches (built from PECD’s official product and from this project’s from-scratch replication respectively) show the same shape, which is strong evidence the effect lives on the SMARD side of the comparison -- wind-speed-driven curtailment and/or high-wind shutdown -- not in either model.

  • Bias by year: stable at ~0.048-0.056 (approach 3) / ~0.014-0.017 (approach 1) for 2019-2022, then steps up to ~0.065-0.072 / ~0.015-0.023 for 2023-2025 -- both approaches step up together, at the same point in time. That’s consistent with a real-world change rather than a modeling artifact specific to either approach: Germany’s reported wind curtailment volumes have risen substantially since 2022-2023 as grid congestion has worsened, which is exactly the growing-curtailment trend this project’s own ../markdown/index.md already flags as expected “to become more common, not less, as renewable penetration grows.”

  • Hour of day: the sharp, localized dip around 10-11 UTC is not a curtailment/oversupply signature after all -- it lines up exactly with a documented ERA5 artifact already called out in this project’s own pecdr/domain.py: “ERA5 has a documented spurious wind-speed dip at [10:00 UTC], which PECD corrects via interpolation as a separate preprocessing step not yet replicated here.” Approach 1 (PECD’s own product, which does apply that correction) shows a much shallower dip at the same hour than approach 3 (this project’s replication, which doesn’t), consistent with this being substantially an uncorrected input-data artifact in our pipeline rather than a distinct physical effect. Concrete, fixable follow-up: interpolate across 10:00 UTC in the raw ERA5 wind speed before running it through the rest of the pipeline, the way PECD itself does.

  • Oversupply proxy: bias climbs with renewable share of load, but this ratio is highly collinear with CF level itself (wind dominates the numerator here), so this panel is largely a restatement of the first result rather than independent evidence for oversupply-driven curtailment specifically.

Net: the CF-level, seasonal, and year-over-year patterns all support treating most of the remaining bias as wind-speed-driven curtailment/ shutdown that neither PECD’s own product nor this replication attempts to model -- which is what makes a CF-conditional statistical correction (e.g. quantile mapping, binned by CF level) a reasonable next step for anyone consuming this as a forecasting feature. The diurnal dip, on the other hand, looks like a specific, fixable ERA5-interpolation gap rather than a modeling limitation to correct for statistically.
