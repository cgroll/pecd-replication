What drives the offshore gap against SMARD?

Offshore mirror of onshore’s 22_analyse_bias_drivers. 34_compare_wind_offshore_approaches found both approach 1 (PECD official x MaStR) and approach 3 (this project’s replication) systematically overshoot SMARD’s actual wind-offshore generation. Since approach 1 isn’t derived from this project’s replication at all, a shared pattern between the two is evidence the gap is a genuine physical-vs-grid effect (curtailment, outages, storm shutdown), not a defect specific to this project’s methodology.

Same five panels as onshore, pooled across pecdr.domain.FULL_YEAR_BACKTEST_YEARS: bias vs. CF level, vs. month, vs. hour of day, vs. a renewable-oversupply proxy, and vs. year.

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

from pecdr.capacity_weighting import capacity_weighted_cf, total_hourly_capacity
from pecdr.domain import FULL_YEAR_BACKTEST_YEARS
from pecdr.paths import ProjPaths

paths = ProjPaths()
paths.ensure_directories()

PERIOD_LABEL = f"{FULL_YEAR_BACKTEST_YEARS[0]}-{FULL_YEAR_BACKTEST_YEARS[-1]}"

Rebuild the same three series as 34_compare_wind_offshore_approaches¶

Approach 1 is restricted to the PEOF zones PECD actually models (DE011_OFF/DE012_OFF/DE02_OFF) -- see 34’s note on the dead-weight-capacity dilution from PECD’s 3 entirely-NaN zones.

mastr_peof_month = pd.read_parquet(paths.mastr_capacity_by_peof_month_file)

pecd_peof_cf = pd.read_parquet(paths.pecd_wind_offshore_capacity_factors_file)
pecd_modeled_zones = pecd_peof_cf.columns[pecd_peof_cf.notna().any()].tolist()
approach1 = capacity_weighted_cf(pecd_peof_cf[pecd_modeled_zones], mastr_peof_month)

approach3 = pd.read_parquet(paths.wind_offshore_full_year_cf_file)["national"]

target_panel = pd.read_parquet(paths.target_panel_file)
total_capacity_hourly = total_hourly_capacity(mastr_peof_month, target_panel.index)
smard_implied_cf = target_panel["wind_offshore"] / total_capacity_hourly

comparison = pd.DataFrame({
    "1_pecd_official_x_mastr": approach1,
    "3_pecd_replication": approach3,
    "4_smard_implied": smard_implied_cf,
}).loc[FULL_YEAR_BACKTEST_YEARS[0]:FULL_YEAR_BACKTEST_YEARS[-1]].dropna()

comparison["diff1"] = comparison["1_pecd_official_x_mastr"] - comparison["4_smard_implied"]
comparison["diff3"] = comparison["3_pecd_replication"] - comparison["4_smard_implied"]

# Renewable-oversupply proxy: (wind + solar) / load, system-wide (not just
# offshore) -- oversupply-driven curtailment depends on the whole system's
# supply/demand balance, not just the offshore zone's own output level.
renewables = target_panel.loc[comparison.index, ["pv", "wind_onshore", "wind_offshore"]].sum(axis=1)
comparison["renewable_share_of_load"] = renewables / target_panel.loc[comparison.index, "load"]

print(f"Hours compared: {len(comparison):,}")
print(f"Approach 1 vs SMARD -- bias: {comparison['diff1'].mean():.4f}, MAE: {comparison['diff1'].abs().mean():.4f}")
print(f"Approach 3 vs SMARD -- bias: {comparison['diff3'].mean():.4f}, MAE: {comparison['diff3'].abs().mean():.4f}")

Takeaways¶

  • CF level: hump-shaped, not monotonic like onshore’s. Bias climbs from ~0 up to a peak around CF~0.5 (+0.13 to +0.22), then falls back toward zero (even slightly negative for approach 1) at the highest CF bins (0.8-0.9) -- unlike onshore, where bias grows roughly monotonically all the way to the top. Two plausible, non-exclusive drivers: PECD’s own product (approach 1) does apply a per-turbine storm-shutdown model in its own methodology (unlike this project’s replication, which dropped it -- see 31’s docstring), which would pull approach 1 down at extreme wind specifically; and this project’s own capacity estimate carries real uncertainty in exactly the direction that would produce this shape (see 35 -- a capacity underestimate at a given hour inflates SMARD_MW / our_capacity right into the highest bins). Both approaches share the reversal, though, which favors something structural over an artifact specific to one methodology -- not fully resolved here.

  • Month: consistent with the curtailment/oversupply story, same direction as onshore -- bias is largest in winter (Jan/Feb, Oct-Dec, +0.10 to +0.14) and smallest in summer (Jun-Aug), though unlike onshore it never approaches zero even in summer (+0.055 at the lowest), tracking offshore’s consistently higher year-round wind resource.

  • Hour of day: the opposite of the naive expectation, and different from onshore. Bias peaks at midday (9-13 UTC, when demand and prices are typically highest) and is lowest overnight (00-05 UTC) -- the reverse of “curtailment happens overnight when demand is low.” The same ERA5 10:00 UTC artifact already identified for onshore (pecdr/domain.py) shows up again here, as a small dip in approach 3 only (approach 3 has no correction for it; approach 1, built from PECD’s own corrected product, doesn’t show it) -- consistent cross-validation of that already-identified, fixable gap, not new evidence either way for curtailment. The midday peak itself is more consistent with solar PV compounding total system oversupply at the same hours offshore wind is already contributing a lot, than with offshore wind curtailment being driven by its own local conditions alone.

  • Oversupply proxy: the cleanest, most monotonic result of the five, and a stronger signal than onshore’s own version of this check. Bias climbs steadily with (PV + wind onshore + wind offshore) / load, all the way to the highest bin (+0.23 to +0.27) -- no reversal at the top, unlike the CF-level panel. Onshore’s equivalent panel was flagged as “largely a restatement” of CF level, since the two are highly collinear there; here they behave genuinely differently (CF-level reverses at the top, this doesn’t), which is more consistent with a real, at least partially independent, system-wide oversupply effect than with offshore’s own wind level alone.

  • Year: bias roughly doubles from a 2019-2021 baseline (+0.05 to +0.08) to a 2023-2024 peak (+0.10 to +0.14), then eases in 2025 (+0.09 to +0.12) -- independently corroborating two other findings already in this project: 34’s yearly bias table (same shape) and 35’s fitted national capacity trajectory (flat 2020-2024, only converging with MaStR’s nameplate figure in 2025). Matches the real-world timeline of Germany’s offshore grid-connection build-out (BorWin5/DolWin6-era platforms) catching up with capacity additions.

Net: three independent diagnostics in this project now (this notebook’s year and oversupply panels, and 35’s capacity-fitting exercise) converge on the same time-varying, system-wide-oversupply-linked story, which is more convincing pooled than any single one of them alone. The CF-level reversal and the midday-peaked diurnal pattern are the two genuinely offshore-specific departures from onshore’s equivalent findings -- both have plausible explanations (storm-shutdown/capacity-ceiling effects at extreme CF; solar-compounded midday oversupply) but neither is nailed down without real curtailment or price data, the natural next step this notebook doesn’t attempt.
