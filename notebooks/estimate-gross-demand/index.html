Estimating gross power demand

Fits equation 7/7a from book/markdown/curtailment_system_equations.md: grid-metered load (D_grid) as a calendar + temperature demand model, minus PV self-consumption, split by MaStR feed-in category. The identification argument: at hours with P_pv(t)=0 (night, heavily overcast), the self-consumption terms vanish and the demand-model coefficients are pinned down with no solar interference at all; daytime hours, where P_pv(t) varies a lot day to day at fixed calendar/weather conditions (cloud cover), then pin down the self-consumption coefficients. One regression, not two sequential models.

Two falsification checks follow directly from the model’s own structure, not from any external data:

  • P_pv,full_feed_in’s coefficient should be statistically indistinguishable from zero -- that capacity is barred from self-consuming by its own feed-in registration.

  • Adding wind potential terms to the same regression should also come back ≈0 -- wind has no behind-the-meter self-consumption to detect.

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf
from patsy import dmatrix  # noqa: F401 -- registers `bs()` for the spline formula used below

paths = ProjPaths()
paths.ensure_directories()

inputs = pd.read_parquet(paths.residual_load_inputs_file)

Falsification check 1: full-feed-in PV should show no self-consumption effect¶

It doesn’t come out at zero -- both PV terms are large and highly significant. Before concluding anything about self-consumption, check whether the split itself is even estimable: P_pv,full_feed_in and P_pv,self_consumption are both national_cf(t) x (a capacity share that only moves month to month) -- if that capacity-mix ratio barely varies within a short window, the two regressors are nearly collinear and OLS can’t reliably separate them.

ffi_coef = model.params["P_pv_full_feed_in_mw"]
ffi_ci = model.conf_int().loc["P_pv_full_feed_in_mw"]
sc_coef = model.params["P_pv_self_consumption_mw"]
sc_ci = model.conf_int().loc["P_pv_self_consumption_mw"]
print(f"full_feed_in coefficient: {ffi_coef:.4f} (95% CI [{ffi_ci[0]:.4f}, {ffi_ci[1]:.4f}]), p={model.pvalues['P_pv_full_feed_in_mw']:.4g}")
print(f"self_consumption coefficient: {sc_coef:.4f} (95% CI [{sc_ci[0]:.4f}, {sc_ci[1]:.4f}]), p={model.pvalues['P_pv_self_consumption_mw']:.4g}")

corr_ffi_sc = inputs["P_pv_full_feed_in_mw"].corr(inputs["P_pv_self_consumption_mw"])

Confirmed: the two regressors correlate at ~0.95. The split (equation 7a) isn’t reliably identified by plain OLS given this data -- there just isn’t enough independent variation between the two series within a short window to separate their individual effects. The sum of the two coefficients (equivalent to fitting the single aggregate P_pv_mw term from equation 7, not the split) is the trustworthy number here.

# Fit the single-aggregate specification directly (equation 7, not 7a):
aggregate_formula = (
    "D_grid_mw ~ C(dow) + C(hour) + is_holiday "
    "+ heating_degree + cooling_degree + years_since_start + P_pv_mw"
)
model_aggregate = smf.ols(aggregate_formula, data=inputs).fit()
k_aggregate = -model_aggregate.params["P_pv_mw"]
print(f"Aggregate self-consumption fraction (equation 7, trustworthy): {k_aggregate:.3f}")
print(f"(For reference, the untrustworthy split summed to: {-(ffi_coef + sc_coef):.3f})")

Ruling out temperature misspecification as the cause¶

Wind potential correlates with heating_degree (~0.15-0.18) -- windier weather tends to be colder weather in Central Europe -- so a first guess is that the simple 2-hinge degree-day term is too rigid and wind is soaking up residual, unmodeled temperature nonlinearity. Testing this directly with a much more flexible temperature response (a 6-df spline instead of two linear hinges) essentially rules it out: both wind coefficients barely move.

spline_formula = (
    "D_grid_mw ~ C(dow) + C(hour) + is_holiday "
    "+ bs(temperature_c, df=6) + years_since_start "
    "+ P_pv_full_feed_in_mw + P_pv_self_consumption_mw + P_wind_on_mw + P_wind_off_mw"
)
model_spline = smf.ols(spline_formula, data=inputs).fit()
comparison = pd.DataFrame({
    "hinge_spec": model_with_wind.params[["P_wind_on_mw", "P_wind_off_mw"]],
    "spline_spec": model_spline.params[["P_wind_on_mw", "P_wind_off_mw"]],
})

Barely different. The more likely explanation is that this is a real effect the falsification test’s own premise didn’t anticipate: wind’s assumed-zero coefficient rests on “no behind-the-meter self-consumption channel,” but there’s a different, entirely legitimate channel wind does have that solar’s full-feed-in category doesn’t need either -- high wind tends to push wholesale prices down, and some price-responsive industrial demand (aluminium smelting and similar) is known to ramp up during cheap/high-renewable hours. That’s a real demand-side response, not a self-consumption artifact, and this regression was never designed to net it out. It’s also a plausible partial confound for the PV self-consumption estimate above, in the opposite direction (if solar has a similar price effect, it should push the PV coefficient toward positive/less-negative, not more negative) -- so the aggregate self-consumption fraction reported above is a reasonable estimate, but likely a slight underestimate of true self-consumption if this channel is real, not an overestimate.

Temperature response shape¶

Should be V-shaped: demand rising as temperature drops below the heating reference (15°C) and rising again above the cooling reference (20°C), both with positive slopes -- a directly falsifiable prediction, not just a curve-fitting artifact.

temp_grid = np.linspace(inputs["temperature_c"].min(), inputs["temperature_c"].max(), 200)
heating_grid = np.clip(15 - temp_grid, 0, None)
cooling_grid = np.clip(temp_grid - 20, 0, None)
temp_response = model.params["heating_degree"] * heating_grid + model.params["cooling_degree"] * cooling_grid

The cooling side comes back essentially flat and statistically insignificant (p=0.92) -- not a modeling failure, but a plausible real feature of the German market: air-conditioning penetration is historically low compared to, say, Southern Europe or the US, so a muted cooling-demand response is a reasonable finding, not a red flag the way the PV/wind checks above are.

Does gross demand fill in the solar self-consumption dip?¶

Average intraday profile, summer (Jun-Aug, high solar) vs. winter (Dec-Feb, minimal solar): D_grid should show a clear midday dip in summer that’s largely absent in winter; D_true_hat (gross demand, PV terms zeroed out) should be much flatter across both seasons if the model is doing its job.

# Uses the aggregate model (the trustworthy specification, given the
# collinearity finding above) rather than the split one -- the two should
# barely differ here anyway, since zeroing out both split terms removes
# the same total self-consumption effect either way.
d_true_hat = model_aggregate.predict(inputs.assign(P_pv_mw=0.0))
profile_df = pd.DataFrame({"D_grid_mw": inputs["D_grid_mw"], "D_true_hat_mw": d_true_hat, "hour": inputs["hour"]}, index=inputs.index)

Takeaways¶

  • Neither falsification check passed cleanly, and that’s more informative than a clean pass would have been. The full-feed-in/ self-consumption PV split isn’t estimable by plain OLS -- the two regressors correlate at ~0.95, since both are national_cf(t) x a capacity-mix ratio that only moves month to month, leaving too little independent variation to separate them. The aggregate specification (equation 7, one P_pv_mw term) is the trustworthy one; the category split (7a) is documented here as a genuine non-result, not silently dropped.

  • Wind’s nonzero coefficient survived a direct test of the obvious alternative explanation (temperature misspecification leaking through a correlate of wind) and is more likely a real, different effect this falsification check’s own premise didn’t anticipate: price-responsive industrial demand rising during cheap, high-wind hours. That’s a legitimate demand-side phenomenon, not an artifact -- but it’s also a plausible reason the PV self-consumption estimate above is a slight underestimate, if a similar (opposite-signed) price effect exists for solar.

  • The temperature response has the expected V-shape on the heating side, and an essentially flat, insignificant cooling side -- plausibly real (Germany’s historically low air-conditioning penetration), not a red flag.

  • D_true_hat visibly fills in the summer midday dip that D_grid shows (the intraday-profile chart) -- the gap between the two peaks at 9-17 UTC specifically, exactly where solar output is highest. In winter the gap is much smaller and roughly flat across the whole day rather than midday-concentrated -- consistent with it being mostly ordinary model-fit residual (R²=0.84, not 1.0) rather than a solar effect, since there’s little sun to self-consume in winter in the first place.

Net: the aggregate self-consumption fraction (k ≈ the value printed above) is this notebook’s headline number, with two honestly-reported caveats (the category split, and the wind confound) rather than a single clean result. Both curtailment estimation (39) and the residual-load backtest (40) use the aggregate D_true_hat from here.
